# Q04: what the repaired network analysis contributes to the DOF story

The existing network analysis reproducibly prioritizes DOFs, and the submitted experiments support YUC5-promoter activity by several family members. The network does **not** establish an endodermal DOF-to-YUC5 pathway. Every one of the ten experimentally tested DOF-to-YUC5 relationships was already supplied in the inference prior; YUC5 was excluded before fitting every endodermal network. DOF enrichment survives the corrected gene background and omission of the two tissues with a target-selection error, but becomes much weaker after accounting for prior coverage. The strongest use of this section is candidate prioritization followed by explicitly bounded experimental evidence.

## Candidate evidence and its limits

The submitted Fig. 6C, physical PDF page 60, reports relative YUC5-promoter activity for ten DOFs in mesophyll protoplasts. DAG1 and OBP3 bind the tested YUC5 promoter fragment in vitro in Fig. 6D/E. Four mutants appear in the RALF23 root-length experiment in Fig. 6F/G. Fig. S14D, physical PDF page 83, tests the ten factors for RALF23-associated transcript induction, using an alternative name for one factor. These assays address different links in the model.

| Factor | Basal atlas context with a current DEG call | Retained DOF-to-YUC5 network edges | Relevant experimental evidence | Unresolved connection |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| DOF4.6 | Maturation endodermis | 3, all phloem | Strong promoter activity; WT RALF transcript induction; mutant growth assay | Endogenous YUC5 induction in the mutant; allele function |
| OBP3 | No current DEG call | 2, procambium | Promoter activity; in vitro binding; mutant growth assay | No significant WT transcript induction shown; endogenous YUC5 response in the mutant |
| DAG1 | Maturation endodermis | 8, pericycle/phloem/procambium | Promoter activity; in vitro binding; WT RALF transcript induction | No mutant growth or endogenous YUC5-induction test in the submitted package |
| CDF4 | Elongation phloem-pole and proliferation xylem-pole pericycle | 0 | Modest promoter activity; WT transcript induction; mutant growth assay | Insertion is drawn downstream of coding stop; null status unresolved; no endogenous YUC5 test |
| COG1 | Transition cortex and xylem-pole pericycle | 5, pericycle/phloem | Promoter activity; mutant growth assay | No significant WT transcript induction shown; endogenous YUC5 response untested |

The full matrix includes OBP1, OBP2, DOF2.2, DOF3.2 and DOF4.1 and preserves the two printed promoter-activity values rather than treating them as independent confirmation. "No current DEG call" and "no retained edge" do not mean no expression, no response, or no regulation. The reported growth contrasts and protoplast/qPCR replication hierarchy have not been repaired by this network analysis. In particular, within-genotype significance is not a genotype-by-treatment test.

**Identifier correction:** AT3G45610 is DOF3.2 in the supplied features file and DOF6 in the supplied Kay TF annotation. Thus Fig. 6C and S14D tested the same factor under two names. OBP1 is AT3G50410 / DOF3.4; COG1 is AT1G29160 / DOF1.5. The matrix uses AGI identifiers and retains aliases. All ten DOF-to-YUC5 relationships are present in the supplied aggregate prior. Eight have retained fitted YUC5 edges in at least one existing context; DOF2.2 and CDF4 have none.

The supplied prior contains 12,210,324 target-TF pairs over 36,709 targets and 1,603 TFs, including 656 possible YUC5 regulators. The fitted networks retain 1,030 YUC5 edges across pericycle, phloem, procambium and two xylem contexts. YUC5 is absent from the input gene universe for cortex, endodermis, both epidermal classes, lateral root cap and columella. Its endodermal relationship was therefore **not tested by this workflow**. Aggregate-prior membership does not identify the particular ATAC, DAP or iGRN source for an edge, or establish promoter occupancy in the assayed root cells. The [prior summary](/Users/tmn23/projects/fer-sc-manuscript-2026/revisions/2026-09-08_routeA_execution/q04_network/results/supplied_prior_summary.json) and [YUC5 prior list](/Users/tmn23/projects/fer-sc-manuscript-2026/revisions/2026-09-08_routeA_execution/q04_network/results/supplied_prior_YUC5_regulators.csv) make this distinction explicit.

## DOF nomination is reproducible, but its meaning is narrower

We exactly reconstructed the published four-metric top100/tail-quantile ranking table and the 355 WT-associated and 343 fer-4-associated TF sets. These are relative ranking labels, not genotype-exclusive expression or activity. Changing only the joined DE annotation leaves every network score, ranking label and nominated gene unchanged.

The original family-enrichment denominator was 1,107 classifiable TFs in the already selected rank table. There are 1,344 classifiable TFs in all score tables and 1,292 classifiable Kay TFs with scores in both genotypes in at least one context, before top100 selection. The latter is the closer match to the nomination opportunity. With this 1,292-gene universe containing 33 DOFs, 19/344 classifiable WT-associated TFs and 23/337 classifiable fer-4-associated TFs are DOFs. Fisher odds ratios are 3.90 and 6.91, with BH-adjusted p values 0.00768 and 0.00000722. This supports retaining the family-level observation. The nine ambiguous PlantTFDB assignments are tabulated; excluding them or using the coauthor DOF definition does not change this conclusion. See the [background sensitivity figure](/Users/tmn23/projects/fer-sc-manuscript-2026/revisions/2026-09-08_routeA_execution/q04_network/figures/02_DOF_enrichment_backgrounds.pdf).

The disconfirming result concerns what that enrichment adds beyond the supplied network prior. On the **same 1,213 complete-case TFs and 32 DOFs in every model**, the crude DOF odds ratios are 4.00 for WT-associated and 7.37 for fer-4-associated nomination. Adjusting only for the log-transformed number of supplied prior targets changes these to 0.66 (95% Wald interval 0.29-1.53) and 1.00 (0.37-2.73). Adding baseline outdegree, baseline expression and number of scored contexts gives 0.56 (0.23-1.39) and 1.13 (0.40-3.21). Thus the attenuation is not caused by excluding genes with missing covariates. These are exploratory gene-level logistic models with correlated covariates and aggregate context summaries. They do not prove an artifact or absence of FER biology. They do show that unadjusted family enrichment alone cannot demonstrate exceptional FER-dependent regulation beyond prior coverage. The [identical-case sensitivity figure](/Users/tmn23/projects/fer-sc-manuscript-2026/revisions/2026-09-08_routeA_execution/q04_network/figures/07_DOF_covariate_sensitivity.pdf) displays all model choices, not only the fully adjusted result.

Individual nominations also depend on the arbitrary top-N and quantile cutoffs. For example, changing top100 to top50 while retaining 10% tails yields 201 WT-associated and 194 fer-4-associated TFs, with Jaccard overlaps 0.487 and 0.504 with the submitted lists. The [sensitivity table](/Users/tmn23/projects/fer-sc-manuscript-2026/revisions/2026-09-08_routeA_execution/q04_network/results/TF_nomination_sensitivity_summary.csv) gives every prespecified 50/100/200 by 5%/10%/20% combination. No replicate-resampling or independent network-fit stability was tested.

## The tissue-selection error is real, but does not erase family nomination

The actual H5AD input gene sets for all ten tissue fits match the original substring-based reconstruction exactly. An explicit map of all 46 observed subgroup labels excludes phloem-pole and xylem-pole pericycle DEGs from the separate phloem and xylem target pools. This removes 349 of 3,359 phloem-input genes and 650 of 3,635 xylem-input genes. These are target-feature effects; the existing cell-selection code already excluded pericycle cells from vascular cell subsets.

Between 7.395% and 11.52% of retained phloem edges, and 18.85% to 22.15% of retained xylem edges, touch those extra targets. YUC5 itself is eligible in xylem only because of the pericycle substring match. Deleting these edges after fitting is a sensitivity analysis, not a corrected network refit. The [target-mapping figure](/Users/tmn23/projects/fer-sc-manuscript-2026/revisions/2026-09-08_routeA_execution/q04_network/figures/03_target_membership_consequences.pdf) and exact membership tables document the scope.

A conservative alternative omitted both affected tissue fits entirely and recalculated the pooled ranking thresholds using the other eight fits. It retained 284 WT-associated and 268 fer-4-associated TFs, including 18 DOFs in each set. DOF enrichment persisted, with BH p values 0.00180 and 0.000311. This is sufficient to show that the family nomination is not created solely by the vascular target error. It does not validate any corrected phloem/xylem coefficient or restore the missing endodermal YUC5 test. A full inference rerun was therefore not initiated for this Route A package.

## What to carry into the resubmission

Retain DOFs as candidates supported by reproducible basal network prioritization and complementary promoter/growth assays. Present the cell-stage expression pattern and the assay matrix together, making clear which links were measured in roots and which in heterologous or in vitro assays. Treat the tissue-specific FER phenotype and RALF-induced YUC5/auxin response as the stronger experimental foundation; the network section does not independently establish the proposed endodermal mechanism.

The most discriminating missing measurement remains RALF-induced endogenous YUC5 in a functionally verified DOF mutant, with an auxin-response competence control. DOF4.6 and OBP3 remain complementary candidates for the previously bounded experimental package: DOF4.6 has induction/promoter/growth evidence, while OBP3 has binding/promoter/growth evidence. Their selection is not justified by calling either an independently discovered endodermal YUC5 regulator. If that future bridge fails, retain the tissue phenotype and promoter findings without a serial DOF-mediated pathway claim.

Original images, well/experiment identities and source growth records were not found in the bounded Resnick FER-directory search described in the research log. This is a search limitation, not proof that those records do not exist. Q05 owns further phenotype recovery and analysis.
