## 3. Findings by question

### Q1 Endodermis edge/score mismatch — confirmed issue; comparison is at the right stage (F01, F03)

Recomputing out- and in-degree from the public `*_network_iGRN.csv` for all 87 genotype-contexts and comparing to `*_gene_score_iGRN.csv`: exactly three fer-4 endodermal clusters disagree (Maturation, Proliferation Domain, Transition Domain); 84 agree gene-for-gene; no exported edge list contains duplicate pairs; every list has 20,000 edges with p < 0.01. My numbers reconcile with the frozen remote-object JSON once its definitions are applied (Maturation: 668 common-gene mismatches + 103 scored-but-edgeless + 6 zero-degree-scored-with-edges = 777; 231 − 6 = 225 edge sources absent from the score table). So the CSV exports and the Resnick object carry the same inconsistency, and CellOracle's `merged_score` is by construction computed from `filtered_links`, so the compared stage is correct.

Two observations narrow the mechanism: `degree_out` still sums to 20,000 in the three bad clusters, so the scores describe *some* filtered 20k graph; and the score node set differs substantially from the edge node set (462–623 scored nodes absent from edges; 671–1,013 edge nodes absent from scores; out-degree correlation 0.39–0.59). A top-20k tie-break cannot produce this. The Codex triage's shared-`./network_analysis/` concurrency hazard is a plausible mechanism; its cross-context comparison found no exact match to another currently exported context, which weakens but does not exclude a collision (the colliding output could be an intermediate that was later overwritten). Which component is stale is unresolved and only decidable by re-filtering the unfiltered raw links on Resnick (`checks/remote_endodermis_refilter_check.py`, read-only, not executed here).

**Exposure (fresh, `checks/results/audit_checks_results.json`, `nominations_per_context.csv`, `DOF_nominations_in_inconsistent_contexts.csv`).** The three contexts supply 325 of 1,105 TF nomination rows; 78 of the 512 GRN-TFs are nominated only there; voiding them would give WT 355→283 TFs (19→17 DOFs) and fer-4 343→276 (23→22 DOFs); 23 of 96 DOF context nominations, 11 of 12 endodermal nominations among the ten assayed DOFs and 7 of 8 populated endodermis tiles in panel c depend on them. These three are also the highest-yield contexts in the whole ranking (114/108/103 nominations vs. next 82; median 20). This coincidence is either genuine endodermal rewiring or an artefact of ranking WT scores against fer-4 scores from a different fit. **These figures describe what is exposed; they do not predict the corrected result.** If the stored scores turn out to be the correct component, score-based nominations need not change at all; if the edges are correct, every exposed membership must be recomputed.

### Q2 Phloem/xylem pole-pericycle admission — confirmed, feature-level, counts exact (F02)

The public SLURM-array script selects *cells* with `contains(tissue) & ~contains("pericycle")` (correct) but selects DE *features* with `cluster_id.str.contains(tissue)` alone, so "Phloem Pole Pericycle" and "Xylem Pole Pericycle" DE genes enter the phloem/xylem target sets. Reconstructing from `sig_de_fil.csv`, the Kay TF list and the saved H5AD universes gives 349 of 3,359 (phloem) and 650 of 3,635 (xylem) genes admitted only via pole-pericycle contexts — the stated numbers exactly — and every tissue's saved universe equals TF ∪ substring-DE ∩ adata.var, confirming the rule rather than inferring it. YUC5 survives a clean phloem universe (DE in metaphloem/CC contexts) but drops out of xylem. Consequence: contaminated targets can occupy retained edges and feed degree scores; post-hoc deletion is not a refit (KNN-imputation PCA, TF candidate set and the top-20k cut depend on the gene set), though per-target ridge coefficients are fitted independently, so the change may be moderate rather than wholesale. 88 of 180 all-context union pairs and 32 of the 33 dashed additions have phloem/xylem support (F14).
